How Much Capital Do You Need to Start With MetaTrader Grey Label?

Explore SolutionsAll Services

It’s usually the first question new brokers ask, and the most frustrating to answer, because the honest reply is “it depends”. But it doesn’t depend on mystery. It depends on a handful of decisions you can make up front: where you’re regulated, how you plan to execute trades, and how quickly you want to grow.

Once those are clear, working out your forex broker startup capital becomes far more manageable. Here’s how to break it down.

01

The Grey Label Itself Is Rarely the Biggest Cost

This surprises a lot of people. They expect the platform to eat most of the budget, then discover it’s often one of the smaller line items.

A grey label costs a fraction of a full MetaQuotes license, which is a big part of its appeal. You’ll usually pay a one-time setup fee and then a monthly fee for the server, license and maintenance. Some providers also charge by number of accounts or trading volume.

Prices vary a lot between providers, so get written quotes from a few. When you build your metatrader grey label budget, plan for at least twelve months of platform fees, not just the setup cost. Running out of money in month six because you only budgeted for launch is a very common way for new brokerages to fail.

02

Regulation Is Usually Where the Big Money Goes

Your regulatory route drives your total capital needs more than anything else.

Minimum Capital Requirements

Every regulator sets a minimum amount of capital your company must hold. These range from relatively modest sums in some offshore jurisdictions to hundreds of thousands of euros or more with stricter European and tier-one regulators. The figure often depends on the type of license too. A broker that holds client positions on its own book is usually required to hold more than one that only passes orders through.

This money isn’t spent, but it is locked up. You can’t use it for marketing or salaries.

Setup and Ongoing Compliance

On top of the capital itself, there are legal fees, application fees, local office or director requirements, audits, compliance officers and annual renewal costs. In stricter jurisdictions, these can easily outweigh the platform costs several times over.

Talk to a specialist lawyer before you settle your budget. The jurisdiction you pick will reshape every other number in your plan.

03

Technology Beyond the Platform

A grey label gives you the trading server. You’ll still need to pay for the rest of your stack.

Liquidity bridge: connects your server to liquidity providers, usually with a setup and monthly fee

Forex CRM and client portal: handles onboarding, KYC, deposits and withdrawals

Risk management tools: essential if you run any B-book flow

Website and hosting: your brand’s front door

Back office and reporting tools: especially if your regulator expects detailed reporting

Some grey label providers bundle a few of these together, which can save money and integration headaches. Check the cost of each piece individually so you know what you’re really paying for.

04

Liquidity and Execution Costs

If you’re running A-book, many liquidity providers ask for a deposit or margin before they’ll give you access. The amount depends on the provider and your expected volume, but it’s money you need to set aside before launch.

If you plan to run B-book or hybrid, you’ll need enough capital to absorb client wins. A few lucky traders in your first month can make a serious dent if your balance sheet is thin. This is one of the most overlooked parts of startup capital, and one of the most dangerous.

05

Payment Processing

Payment processors that work with forex often ask for a rolling reserve, holding back a share of deposits for a set period as protection against chargebacks. Some also want an upfront security deposit.

That means part of your clients’ deposits won’t reach your operating account straight away. Plan your cash flow around it.

06

Team and Operations

Even a lean brokerage needs people. At minimum, you’ll usually need:

A compliance officer, often a regulatory requirement

Someone to manage the dealing desk and risk

Customer support covering trading hours

Back office staff for payments and account queries

Sales or account managers, if you’re working with direct clients

Some of this can be outsourced at first, but salaries and contractor fees add up quickly. Budget at least a year of running costs here.

07

Marketing: The Cost People Underestimate

You can have a perfect setup and no clients. Marketing in forex is competitive and expensive, and many ad platforms restrict financial services advertising, which pushes costs up further.

Common channels include IB and affiliate partnerships, content marketing and SEO, paid ads where permitted, events and expos, and social media. IB programmes are often the most cost-effective at the start, because you pay mainly on results.

Whatever channels you choose, give marketing a real budget. Brokers who pour everything into setup and leave nothing for growth rarely get past their first year.

08

Putting a Budget Together

Rather than chasing one magic number, build your budget in layers:

Regulatory capital: the amount your chosen jurisdiction requires you to hold

Setup costs: licensing, legal, grey label setup, integrations, website

Twelve months of running costs: platform fees, tech subscriptions, staff, office, compliance

Liquidity and risk buffer: deposits for liquidity providers, plus a cushion for B-book exposure

Marketing: a realistic first-year budget for bringing in clients

Contingency: an extra margin for delays and surprises, because there will be some

Add those together and you have a figure grounded in your actual plans rather than a guess.

09

Ways to Keep Initial Capital Lower

If the total looks daunting, there are sensible ways to reduce it:

Start in a jurisdiction with lower capital requirements and expand later

Launch with a smaller set of instruments and account types

Choose a provider that bundles the server, CRM and bridge

Rely on IB partnerships instead of heavy paid marketing at first

Outsource compliance, support or dealing until volume justifies hiring

What you shouldn’t cut is your risk buffer or your runway. Those are what keep you alive while you build.

10

Conclusion

Your forex broker startup capital depends mostly on regulation, execution model and growth plans. The grey label is an important piece, but usually not the heaviest. Build your metatrader grey label budget around a full year of operations, keep a healthy buffer, and you’ll give your brokerage a real chance to grow.
