Comparing MetaTrader Grey Label Providers: Features, Support and Prices

Explore SolutionsAll Services

On paper, most grey label providers look almost the same. Dedicated server, admin access, branded terminals, round-the-clock support. Read five websites back to back and you’d struggle to tell them apart.

The differences show up later. They appear when your server slows down during a news spike, when you need a plugin installed urgently, or when you find a fee in month three that nobody mentioned. So when brokers ask us about the best metatrader grey label providers, the honest answer is that it depends less on the brochure and more on what happens once you’re live.

Here’s how to compare providers properly, beyond the sales pitch.

01

Start With the Basics: Are They Legitimate?

Before you look at features or price, check the foundation. A grey label runs under your provider’s master MetaQuotes license. If their license has problems, so do you.

Ask how long they’ve held the license and whether it’s in good standing. Ask how many brokers they currently host and how long their oldest clients have been with them. A provider that’s been around for years with brokers who stay is a far safer bet than one with flashy pricing and no track record.

Also find out what happens if the provider runs into trouble. Can your server and client data be moved? Is there a clear exit process? You’d hope never to need it, but you want the answer before you sign.

02

Comparing Features

When you compare metatrader grey label offers, set up a simple table and score each provider against the same list. It stops one impressive feature from distracting you from gaps elsewhere.

Server and Infrastructure

Which platforms they offer: MT5 only, or MT4 as well where it’s still possible

Where they can host your server, and whether you get to choose

Whether a backup server is included or charged separately

How failover works if the main server goes down

Who handles MetaQuotes updates and how quickly they’re rolled out

Access and Control

Check that you’re actually getting full administrator access, not a limited admin role with some settings locked. Some providers quietly restrict what you can change. Ask directly which settings you can’t touch.

Also find out how many manager terminals are included. A growing dealing desk, support team and back office can need more than you’d think.

Integrations and Plugins

Find out which bridges, CRMs, risk tools and liquidity providers they’ve already integrated. A provider with tested connections saves you weeks of troubleshooting. One that says “we can integrate anything” without examples may be learning on your server.

Ask too whether you can bring your own tools, or whether they push you towards their in-house products.

03

Comparing Support

Support is where providers differ most, and where it’s hardest to judge before you sign.

What to Ask

What hours is technical support actually available, including weekends before the market opens?

Is there live monitoring of your server, or do they only respond once you report a problem?

What are their response times for urgent issues, and are they written into the agreement?

Will you have a named account manager, or will each ticket go to whoever is free?

Do they help with configuration, such as setting up groups and symbols, or is that entirely on you?

How to Test It

Don’t just take their word for it. Send a few technical questions before signing and see how quickly and clearly they reply. Ask for references from current brokers, and actually call them. Ask those brokers about the worst problem they’ve had and how the provider dealt with it. That one answer will tell you more than any sales call.

04

Comparing Prices

Grey label pricing is rarely as simple as one monthly number. Providers bundle and split costs in different ways, so lining up headline prices side by side can mislead you.

Common Cost Elements

One-time setup fee

Monthly server and license fee

Charges per active account, or based on trading volume

Backup server fees

Extra manager terminals

Plugin and integration costs

Charges for custom development or configuration work

Reading Between the Lines

A low setup fee sometimes comes with high volume-based charges, which can get expensive once you start growing. A higher flat fee might work out cheaper over two or three years. Ask each provider to estimate your total cost at three points: at launch, at your expected volume after a year, and at a stretch target. That gives you a far more honest comparison than the opening price.

Also check the contract length and notice period. A cheap deal that locks you in for three years with a heavy exit fee isn’t really cheap.

05

Red Flags to Watch For

A few warning signs come up again and again:

Pricing that changes depending on who you speak to

Vague answers about who actually holds the MetaQuotes license

No clear process for moving your data if you leave

Pressure to sign quickly because of a “limited offer”

Reluctance to put you in touch with existing clients

Support contacts that turn out to be a single email address

Any one of these is worth questioning. Several together usually mean walking away.

06

Matching the Provider to Your Brokerage

The best metatrader grey label providers for a new broker aren’t necessarily the best for an established one. If you’re just starting, you might value hands-on configuration help and a simple, predictable fee. If you’re already running volume, deep integrations, strong risk tools and solid uptime guarantees matter more.

Be honest about where you are now and where you expect to be in a year. Pick the provider that fits that path, not the one with the longest feature list.

07

Conclusion

When you compare metatrader grey label providers, look past the website and test what matters: the license behind the service, the support you’ll get when things go wrong, and the full cost over time. A careful week of comparison can save you a painful migration later.
