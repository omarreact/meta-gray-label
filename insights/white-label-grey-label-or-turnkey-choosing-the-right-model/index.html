White Label, Grey Label or Turnkey? Choosing the Right Model

Spend a little time researching how to start a brokerage and you’ll run into three terms over and over: white label, grey label and turnkey. They’re often presented as three options on the same menu. They aren’t, quite. White label and grey label describe how you access the trading platform. Turnkey describes how much of the whole business comes ready-made.

Once you see it that way, the choice gets much easier. Here’s how the three fit together, and how to work out which one suits your plans.

01

White Label: Renting a Branded Space

A white label puts your brand on a MetaTrader server that someone else runs. You share that server with other brokers, and the host controls the core configuration. You get a manager terminal, branded client terminals and the ability to open accounts and manage clients.

It’s the quickest and cheapest way to get a platform running. The trade-off is limited control over liquidity, execution settings, plugins and data. For brokers testing a market or working with a very tight budget, that trade-off is often worth it.

02

Grey Label: Your Own Server, Someone Else’s License

A grey label gives you a dedicated MetaTrader server running under your provider’s master license. You get admin access, so you configure groups, symbols, swaps and execution yourself. You pick your liquidity providers, add the plugins you want, and your client data sits on your own server.

It costs more than a white label and needs more technical know-how to run, but it gives you far more freedom. Many brokers move to a grey label once they’ve outgrown a white label, while others start there because they already have the experience and client base to use the extra control.

03

Turnkey: The Whole Business in One Package

A turnkey forex broker solution is about scope rather than platform access. Instead of buying the trading server alone and sourcing everything else separately, you get most of what you need to launch from one provider.

A typical turnkey package might include:

The trading platform, on either a white label or grey label basis

A forex CRM and branded client portal

Liquidity access and a bridge

Payment gateway integrations

Risk management and back office tools

Website design and development

Help with company formation and licensing

Ongoing technical support

The idea is simple: one contract, one point of contact and one integrated stack, rather than six vendors who have never worked together.

04

How They Combine

Here’s where people get confused. Turnkey isn’t a third type of platform. It’s a package that sits on top of one.

So you might come across a white label turnkey solution, which is fast and inexpensive with limited control. Or you might choose a metatrader grey label turnkey setup, where you get your own server with admin access plus the CRM, bridge, payments and website already connected around it.

The second option is popular with brokers who want real control over their trading environment but don’t want to spend months stitching together tools from different suppliers.

05

Weighing Up the Options

If Speed and Low Cost Come First

A white label, possibly as part of a basic turnkey bundle, gets you live fastest with the smallest upfront spend. It’s a reasonable way to test demand, learn the business and build an initial client base.

Just go in knowing you’ll probably outgrow it, and check how easy it is to move on later.

If Control Matters Most

A grey label gives you control over execution, liquidity and configuration. If you have an experienced team and clear views on how you want to run your book, sourcing each component yourself lets you pick the best tool for every job.

That said, it takes time and technical effort, and you become the one responsible for making everything talk to everything else.

If You Want Control Without the Assembly

A grey label turnkey package is the middle path. You keep the server control that makes a grey label worthwhile, while your provider handles integration and delivers a working brokerage instead of a box of parts.

For many first-time brokers with a solid budget, this is the most practical route. It’s also common among established businesses expanding into forex from related fields, such as crypto exchanges or financial education companies, who know their audience but not brokerage technology.

06

Questions That Point You the Right Way

Answering these honestly usually settles the choice:

How much capital do you have for setup and your first year of running costs?

Does your team include people who have run a MetaTrader server before?

Do you already have liquidity relationships, or do you need introductions?

How quickly do you need to launch?

Do you already own any parts of the stack, like a CRM or website, that you want to keep?

How much control do you need over execution and risk from day one?

If you’re short on time, technical staff and existing tools, turnkey makes sense. If you have strong opinions and an experienced team, assembling your own stack around a grey label may suit you better.

07

What to Watch For With Turnkey Packages

Turnkey solutions can be excellent, but they come with their own risks:

Lock-in: if everything comes from one provider, leaving can be harder. Check whether you can swap out individual components later.

Hidden weak spots: a package might include an excellent platform but a mediocre CRM. Assess each part, not just the overall bundle.

Vague licensing help: “assistance with licensing” can mean anything from a full application service to a list of law firms. Ask exactly what’s included.

Bundled pricing: ask for a breakdown so you can see what each part really costs.

08

There’s No Permanent Choice

Plenty of brokers start on one model and switch later. A white label turnkey can become a grey label as the business grows. A grey label turnkey can be gradually unbundled as you bring parts of the stack in-house. What matters is choosing something that fits where you are now, with a clear route to where you want to be.

09

Conclusion

White label and grey label decide how much control you have over your trading server. Turnkey decides how much of the rest of the business arrives ready to go. A turnkey forex broker setup built on a grey label gives many brokers the best of both: real control and a much faster, simpler launch.
