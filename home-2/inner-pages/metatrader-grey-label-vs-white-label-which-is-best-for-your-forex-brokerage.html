MetaTrader Grey Label vs White Label: Which Is Best for Your Forex Brokerage?

Practical perspectives on brokerage technology and operations.

5 min read · Meta Grey Label

The context

Most brokers don’t start out asking about server architecture. They start with a budget, a launch date, and a rough sense that they need MetaTrader because that’s what their traders already use. Then someone mentions grey label, someone else says white label, and the conversation gets murky fast.

So let’s untangle it. The MetaTrader grey label vs white label question isn’t really about which one is better. It comes down to how much of your brokerage you want to own on day one, and how much you’re happy to rent.

01 / Explore the detail

A Quick Bit of Background First

MetaQuotes, the company behind MT4 and MT5, sells full server licenses directly. They’re expensive and come with strict requirements, and new MT4 licenses are no longer issued at all. That gap is why white label and grey label models exist. Providers who already hold full licenses carve out access and offer it to newer brokers at a much lower entry cost.

Both routes put your brand on the terminal your clients log into. The difference is in what’s happening behind that login screen.

02 / Explore the detail

What a White Label Actually Gives You

With a white label, you’re basically a tenant. The provider runs the server, and your brokerage sits on it as a branded segment alongside other brokers. Your clients see your name and logo. You get a manager terminal, reporting tools, and the ability to open accounts and set up some trading groups.

01

Where White Label Works Well

Speed is the big one. A white label can often go live in a couple of weeks because there’s nothing to build. It’s cheaper too, both upfront and month to month, which matters a lot when you’re still finding out whether your marketing can bring in funded clients.

It also suits brokers who’d rather not think about infrastructure at all. The provider handles uptime, backups, security patches and plugin updates.

02

Where It Starts to Pinch

The trade-off is control. You’re working inside someone else’s setup. Want a bridge to a liquidity provider they don’t support? You might wait, or you might go without. Need a custom plugin, or a different approach to execution and risk? That’s usually something you have to request, not something you decide.

Then there’s the shared server. If the host has an outage, every broker on it has one too. And your client data sits on infrastructure you don’t control, which can make some regulators and institutional partners uneasy.

03 / Explore the detail

What Changes With a Grey Label

A grey label sits in the middle ground between a white label and a full license. You get your own dedicated MetaTrader server instance, usually MT5 these days. It runs under the provider’s master license but is kept separate from other brokers.

In practice, that means admin access, not just manager access. You configure symbols, groups, swaps, margin rules and execution settings yourself. You pick your liquidity providers and bridges, and you can install plugins that fit how you want to run your book. Your client data stays on your own server.

01

Who Grey Label Suits

Brokers who already have some traction usually get the most out of it. If you have a client base, a clear plan for A-book, B-book or hybrid execution, and a team that knows its way around a MetaTrader admin panel, a grey label lets you run things your way.

It’s also a sensible next step if you’ve outgrown a white label but aren’t ready for a full license, or can’t get one.

02

The Extra Cost and Work

More control means more work. Setup and monthly fees are higher than for a white label. You’ll need people, in-house or outsourced, who can manage the server, watch risk and keep the configuration clean. Launch also takes longer because there’s real setup involved.

04 / Explore the detail

The Part That Hits Your Margins

This is where the difference grey vs white label setups make really shows: how much room you have to shape your revenue.

On a white label, spreads, markups, commission structures and hedging all sit within limits the host sets. On a grey label, you set those limits yourself. That doesn’t make it automatically more profitable. A badly configured grey label can lose money faster than a well-run white label ever would. But the ceiling is higher, and the decisions are yours.

A quick side-by-side:

Control: white label gives you manager access, grey label gives you admin access to your own server

Liquidity: white label ties you to the host’s options, grey label lets you choose

Launch time: white label is usually quicker

Cost: white label is cheaper to start and to run

Data: on a white label it sits on a shared server, on a grey label it sits on yours

05 / Explore the detail

So Which One Should You Pick?

Honestly, it depends on where your brokerage is today, not where you hope it’ll be in three years.

If you’re new, testing a market or working with a tight budget, a white label is usually the smarter place to start. You get to market quickly, see what your clients actually trade, and keep costs under control. Plenty of successful brokers started this way and moved on later.

If you already have clients, a defined execution model and some technical depth in-house, a grey label starts to make more sense. The extra cost earns its keep once the host’s restrictions are costing you money or clients.

Before you decide, it’s worth asking yourself a few things:

How many funded clients do you realistically expect in the first year?

Do you have someone who can manage a MetaTrader server, or will you need to hire?

Are you tied to particular liquidity providers?

What does your regulator expect around data storage and server control?

If most of your answers are “not yet” or “not sure,” start with a white label. If they’re “yes, and it’s already a problem,” look seriously at a grey label. In the end, the difference grey vs white label models come down to is simple: who holds the keys.

06 / Explore the detail

Conclusion

The MetaTrader grey label vs white label decision isn’t permanent. Lots of brokers launch on a white label, build volume, then move to a grey label once they need more control. That path is perfectly reasonable, and often cheaper than jumping straight into infrastructure you’re not ready to run.
