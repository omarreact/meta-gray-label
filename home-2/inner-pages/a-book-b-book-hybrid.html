The context

Ask any new broker what keeps them up at night and execution often comes up. Not because it’s complicated to set up, but because the choice you make shapes your revenue, your risk and how your clients experience every trade.

The a book vs b book debate has been running in this industry for years, and plenty of people have strong opinions. This page sets out how each model works, the trade-offs involved and why so many brokers now settle on a hybrid. The aim isn’t to tell you which is right. It’s to help you choose with your eyes open.

01 / Explore the detail

What Is A Book Execution?

With a book execution, client orders are passed straight through to your liquidity providers. When a client buys EUR/USD, your broker places a matching trade with the market. You’re the middle person, not the counterparty.

01

How A Book Brokers Make Money

Revenue comes from spread markups and commissions on each trade. Whether the client wins or loses, you earn from their trading activity.

02

The Advantages

There’s no direct conflict of interest, since you don’t profit when clients lose. Your market risk stays low because positions are offset with providers. And some professional traders and institutional clients specifically look for brokers running this model.

03

The Downsides

Margins per trade are thinner. You need decent volumes to build meaningful revenue. Your costs depend on what your liquidity providers charge, and some flow, like aggressive scalping, can be expensive to pass on.

02 / Explore the detail

What Is a B Book Forex Broker?

A b book forex broker keeps client trades in-house rather than sending them to the market. The broker becomes the counterparty. If a client buys, the broker effectively takes the opposite side.

01

How B Book Brokers Make Money

Revenue comes from spreads and commissions, plus net client losses. Statistically, a large share of retail traders lose over time, which is why this model can be profitable.

02

The Advantages

Higher potential revenue per client. No liquidity costs on internalised trades. Faster execution, since orders don’t leave your server.

03

The Downsides

There’s a built-in conflict of interest, because you benefit when clients lose. Profitable traders become a real cost and risk. A run of winning clients, or a sharp market move, can hurt your balance sheet. And regulators in many jurisdictions watch B book practices closely, so transparency and fair treatment are essential.

04

Doing It Responsibly

Running a B book doesn’t have to mean treating clients unfairly. Responsible brokers execute honestly, never manipulate prices, disclose their execution model and keep enough capital to cover exposure. Reputation in this industry travels fast.

03 / Explore the detail

How the Forex Hybrid Model Works

The forex hybrid model combines both approaches. Some client flow goes to liquidity providers. Some stays in-house. The split is decided by rules your dealing team sets.

01

Typical Ways to Split Flow

By client type, so consistently profitable traders are routed out and smaller retail accounts stay internal. By trade size, so large orders go to the market to limit exposure. By instrument, so certain volatile symbols are always hedged. Or by overall exposure, so once internal risk reaches a set level, new trades are passed through.

02

Why Many Brokers Choose Hybrid

It balances revenue with risk. You keep the income potential of internalising some flow, while protecting yourself from the clients and positions that could cause real damage. It’s flexible too, so you can adjust as your client base changes.

03

What It Requires

A hybrid model only works with good data and the right tools. Your dealing team needs to see client behaviour, exposure and profitability clearly, and adjust routing rules confidently.

04 / Explore the detail

A Book vs B Book: Side-by-Side Thinking

Rather than a simple table, it helps to ask a few questions about your own business.

How much capital do you have to absorb risk? A B book needs more. What kind of clients are you targeting? Professional and high-volume traders tend to prefer A book. What do your regulators expect? Some jurisdictions are stricter than others. Do you have an experienced dealing team? B book and hybrid models depend on one. How important is reputation in your market? Transparency carries more weight in some regions than in others.

Your honest answers usually point clearly towards one model or another.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which model is best for a new brokerage?

Many new brokers start with A book or a cautious hybrid, as it limits risk while they learn their client base. B book requires more capital and experience to manage safely.

Is B book execution legal?

In many jurisdictions it’s permitted, but it often comes with requirements around disclosure, capital and fair treatment of clients. Your legal advisor should confirm what applies to your licence and markets.

Can I change my execution model later?

Yes. Routing rules sit in your bridge and risk tools, so you can shift from A book to hybrid, or adjust your hybrid split, as your business evolves.

Do clients know which model I use?

They should. Many regulators expect brokers to disclose their execution approach, and being open about it builds trust, especially with experienced traders.
