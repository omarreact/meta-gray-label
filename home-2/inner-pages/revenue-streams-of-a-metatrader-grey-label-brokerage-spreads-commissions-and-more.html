Revenue Streams of a MetaTrader Grey Label Brokerage: Spreads, Commissions and More

Practical perspectives on brokerage technology and operations.

6 min read · Meta Grey Label

The context

New brokers often build their business plan around one idea: clients trade, we take a small cut, repeat. It’s a reasonable starting point, but it misses most of what separates a brokerage that scrapes by from one that does well. The money comes in through several doors, and each one behaves differently when markets go quiet, when a big client leaves, or when a regulator changes the rules.

Running a grey label puts the controls for most of those doors in your hands. So it pays to know exactly what each one is and how to set it.

01 / Explore the detail

Why Control Over Pricing Changes Everything

On a white label, a lot of your pricing is shaped by the host. You might be able to tweak spreads within a range, but execution, swaps and group settings often come pre-set.

A grey label is different. With admin access to your own MetaTrader server, you decide what clients pay and how their trades are handled. That’s what makes it possible to design a revenue model around your actual clients instead of someone else’s defaults. It’s also why forex broker revenue on a grey label can look quite different from one brokerage to the next, even when they use similar technology.

02 / Explore the detail

The Markup on Every Price

Your liquidity provider streams you prices. You show clients slightly different ones. The gap between them is your markup, and it’s the most familiar source of income in the industry.

What many brokers overlook is how precisely this can be tuned. Rather than adding the same markup everywhere, you can:

Keep majors like EURUSD and GBPUSD sharp, since traders compare them constantly

Price exotics and less liquid instruments more generously, where clients expect wider spreads

Set different markups for beginner accounts, VIP accounts and IB-referred groups

A small change across a busy symbol adds up quickly. Equally, getting greedy on the pairs everyone watches is the fastest way to lose experienced traders.

03 / Explore the detail

Charging Per Trade Instead

Not every trader wants costs hidden in the spread. Scalpers, algorithmic traders and high-volume clients usually prefer raw pricing and a visible commission per lot. They can calculate exactly what each trade costs, which matters a great deal for their strategies.

Offering a commission-based account can bring in clients who would never consider a standard account. On your grey label server, you can set commissions by group, scale them down for higher volumes, or create special terms for professional clients and money managers.

01

Picking the Right Mix

Most brokers offer both. A standard account with no commission and slightly wider spreads works for newer or occasional traders. A raw account with commission suits the serious crowd. Running both lets you serve each group without forcing either into a model they dislike.

04 / Explore the detail

Overnight Positions

Clients who keep trades open past the daily rollover pay or earn swap, based on the interest rate gap between two currencies. Brokers add a margin on top of the base rate.

It’s quiet money, rarely noticed by short-term traders but steady across longer-term positions. Since you set swap rates yourself on a grey label, you can stay competitive on popular pairs while earning a fair amount elsewhere.

If you offer swap-free accounts, plan them carefully. A fixed holding fee after a certain number of days is common, but the terms need to be transparent and applied consistently.

05 / Explore the detail

Taking the Other Side

This is the part of brokerage income people argue about most.

When you keep a client’s trade in-house rather than passing it to the market, you become the counterparty. Their loss is your gain, and their gain is your cost. This is known as B-booking.

Many brokers combine approaches. Large, consistently profitable or fast-trading clients go to liquidity providers. Smaller, less experienced flow stays internal. Handled with discipline, this hybrid approach can be a major contributor to metatrader grey label profit.

The word “discipline” is doing a lot of work there. Without proper risk tools, clear routing rules and someone watching exposure in real time, B-book revenue can turn negative very quickly. A grey label gives you the access to set all of this up yourself, but it doesn’t remove the risk.

06 / Explore the detail

Income From Copy Trading and Managed Money

Copy trading, PAMM and MAM accounts add another layer. You can earn a share of performance or subscription fees charged by strategy providers and money managers.

The bigger effect, though, is volume. A single trader with a few hundred followers turns one decision into hundreds of trades. Each of those carries spread, commission or swap revenue, which can make a well-run copy trading platform one of the more valuable parts of the business.

07 / Explore the detail

Smaller Streams That Still Count

Beyond trading, a few extras can help:

VPS hosting for clients running EAs around the clock

Paid education, mentoring or premium webinars

Signal services and advanced analytics tools

New instruments such as share CFDs, commodities or crypto, where permitted

Individually they’re modest. Together, they can noticeably improve your margins and make your offering feel more complete.

08 / Explore the detail

Fees: Use Sparingly

Inactivity fees, withdrawal charges and conversion fees can bring in some revenue, but they come at a cost to trust. Traders are quick to share stories of brokers who nickel-and-dime them. Many brokers now treat zero-fee deposits and withdrawals as part of their marketing and earn their money through trading instead. If you do charge anything, keep it modest and spell it out clearly.

09 / Explore the detail

Where the Money Goes Out

Revenue isn’t profit. Before you can judge whether your model works, account for what flows the other way:

Grey label, bridge, CRM and other technology fees

Liquidity and execution costs

Payment processor fees and rolling reserves

IB rebates and affiliate payouts

Salaries, compliance, audits and office costs

Marketing and acquisition spend

B-book losses during periods when clients do well

IB rebates often surprise new brokers. Generous payouts can absorb most of the spread revenue from referred clients, so set rebate levels with your margins in mind, not just to win partners.

10 / Explore the detail

Don’t Lean on One Stream

Brokerages that rely heavily on a single source of income are fragile. A regulator restricting leverage, a key IB leaving, or a long stretch of calm markets can hit hard.

The more resilient approach spreads income across markups, commissions, swaps, carefully managed internal flow, copy trading volume and a few value-added services. When one slows, the others keep the lights on.

11 / Explore the detail

Conclusion

Steady forex broker revenue doesn’t come from squeezing clients. It comes from pricing each product thoughtfully, managing risk properly and building several income sources that support each other. A grey label gives you direct access to every one of those levers, and that’s where real metatrader grey label profit starts.
